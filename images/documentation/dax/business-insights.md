The analysis identified several important performance and risk issues across sales, collections, receivables and profitability.

1. Port Harcourt required the greatest collection attention
Port Harcourt recorded approximately:
₦10.965M in Gross Sales
₦7.561M in Collections
₦3.404M Outstanding
68.96% Collection Rate
85% Collection Target
This represented a negative collection-rate variance of approximately 16.04 percentage points against target.
Among the three branches, Port Harcourt showed the weakest collection performance relative to expectations.
Management implication:
Collection activity in Port Harcourt required stronger attention, including review of overdue accounts, customer follow-up and branch-level collection processes.

2. Receivables ageing represented a significant financial risk
Total outstanding receivables were approximately:
₦8.69M
Of this amount:
approximately ₦4.321M was more than 90 days overdue;
approximately ₦6.615M was more than 60 days overdue.
This showed that the receivables problem was not simply the amount outstanding. A significant proportion had remained unpaid for extended periods.
Management implication:
Older receivables should be prioritised for collection because prolonged ageing may reduce the likelihood of recovery and negatively affect cash flow.

3. Corporate customers represented the greatest receivables exposure
The Corporate customer segment had approximately:
₦5.041M Outstanding
with a Collection Rate of approximately:
65.44%
Corporate customers also represented 6 of the Top 10 debtors.
Management implication:
Although Corporate customers may contribute significant sales volume, the segment also represented the greatest collection risk. Management should review payment terms, credit limits and collection follow-up for high-exposure Corporate accounts.

4. High sales did not necessarily mean stronger profitability efficiency
The Conference Room Projector generated approximately:
₦8.4M in Sales
and approximately:
₦2.1M in Gross Margin
However, its Gross Margin % was approximately:
25%
By comparison, the Executive Desk generated lower sales of approximately:
₦4.08M
but achieved a higher Gross Margin % of approximately:
30.83%
Management implication:
Product performance should not be assessed using revenue alone. Management should consider both absolute contribution and margin efficiency when making pricing, sales and product-mix decisions.

5. Sales performance varied significantly across the six-month period
Sales increased strongly between January and February, with February recording approximately:
39% Month-on-Month growth
Performance then weakened in March and declined sharply in April by approximately:
44% Month-on-Month
Sales began recovering gradually in May and June.
Management implication:
Management should investigate both the drivers of February’s strong performance and the factors behind the sharp April decline to understand whether the movement was caused by customer activity, product mix, branch performance, seasonality or operational factors.

Overall Business Interpretation
The analysis showed that management performance could not be understood from sales alone.
The most important issues emerged when sales were examined alongside:
Collections → Receivables Ageing → Customer Risk → Branch Targets → Profitability → Monthly Trends
The findings highlighted a need for stronger collection management, particularly around older Corporate receivables and Port Harcourt branch performance, while also demonstrating the importance of evaluating profitability alongside revenue growth.
