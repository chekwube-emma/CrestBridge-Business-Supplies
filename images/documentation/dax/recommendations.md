Recommendations & Management Actions

Based on the analysis, the following actions would help management improve collections, reduce receivables risk and strengthen performance monitoring.

1. Prioritise Port Harcourt collection recovery
Port Harcourt recorded the weakest collection performance relative to target.
Management should:
review the largest overdue accounts in the branch;
assign targeted collection actions to high-risk customers;
monitor collection performance against the 85% branch target more frequently;
investigate whether the weak collection rate is driven by customer mix, payment terms or follow-up processes.

2. Introduce ageing-based collection priorities
Because a large proportion of receivables was already more than 60 and 90 days overdue, collection efforts should be prioritised by ageing severity.
A practical approach would be to classify receivables into action groups such as:
current / low risk;
31–60 days;
61–90 days;
over 90 days.
Accounts in the oldest categories should receive the highest level of follow-up.

3. Review Corporate customer credit exposure
Corporate customers represented the largest receivables exposure and accounted for most of the Top 10 debtors.
Management should consider:
reviewing payment terms for high-exposure Corporate accounts;
monitoring credit limits;
identifying repeat late-paying customers;
escalating long-overdue accounts earlier;
assessing whether continued sales should be restricted for severely overdue customers.

4. Monitor profitability alongside revenue
Management should avoid evaluating product performance using sales value alone.
Products should be reviewed using both:
absolute Gross Margin;
Gross Margin %.
This would help management identify products that contribute strongly to total profit as well as products that deliver stronger margin efficiency.

5. Investigate significant monthly sales movements
The sharp increase in February and significant decline in April should be investigated further.
Management should compare these periods across:
branch;
customer segment;
major customers;
product category;
high-value orders.
This would help determine whether the changes were driven by one-off transactions, operational issues, customer behaviour or broader sales trends.

6. Establish routine management reporting
The dashboard should be used as part of a recurring management review process rather than as a one-time analytical exercise.
A regular reporting cycle could include:
sales performance;
collections;
outstanding balances;
ageing;
branch target performance;
high-risk customers;
profitability;
month-on-month movement.
This would allow emerging issues to be identified earlier and management actions to be tracked over time.
Recommended Management Focus
The immediate priority should be:
Improve collections → reduce ageing exposure → manage Corporate receivables → monitor branch targets → protect profitability
The reporting solution provides the structure needed to monitor these areas consistently and support follow-up decisions.
