KPI Definitions

This section explains the main KPIs and measures used in the CrestBridge Business Supplies Power BI project.

Valid Gross Sales What it measures: The total sales value generated from transactions classified as valid and eligible for financial reporting.

Why it matters:
It ensures that reported sales are based only on transactions that passed the defined data-quality and reporting rules.

Amount Collected What it measures: The total amount of customer payments received against valid sales transactions.

Why it matters:
It shows how much of reported sales has actually been converted into cash and supports collection-performance monitoring.

Total Outstanding Balance What it measures:
The total value of unpaid balances remaining after customer payments are deducted from valid sales.

Why it matters:
It provides management with visibility into receivables exposure and helps identify the amount of revenue still awaiting collection.

Collection Rate What it measures:
The percentage of valid sales that has been collected.

Why it matters:
It shows how effectively the business is converting sales into cash and helps compare collection performance across branches, periods and customer groups.

Total Gross Margin What it measures:
The total profit remaining after deducting the cost of goods sold from valid sales.

Why it matters:
It helps management understand the absolute profit contribution generated by products, categories and the overall business.

Overall Gross Margin % What it measures:
Gross margin expressed as a percentage of sales.

Why it matters:
It shows profitability efficiency and helps distinguish between products that generate high sales and those that generate stronger margins.

Branch Collection Target What it measures:
The expected collection-rate target assigned to each branch.

Why it matters:
It provides a benchmark against which actual branch collection performance can be evaluated.

Collection Rate Variance vs Target What it measures:
The difference between the actual collection rate and the branch collection target.

Why it matters:
It shows whether a branch is performing above or below its expected collection level and highlights areas requiring management attention.

Collection Target Attainment % What it measures: The extent to which a branch has achieved its collection target, expressed as a percentage.

Why it matters:
It provides a simple way to compare target achievement across branches and identify underperforming locations.

Month-on-Month Sales Change What it measures:
The percentage increase or decrease in valid sales compared with the previous month.

Why it matters:
It helps management identify changes in sales momentum, unusual performance movements and periods that require further investigation.

High Value Orders What it measures:
Orders that meet or exceed a defined value threshold and are therefore considered financially significant.

Why it matters:
It helps management identify transactions that have a greater impact on sales, collections and receivables exposure.

Collection Priority What it measures:
A prioritisation measure used to identify customer accounts requiring stronger collection attention based on factors such as outstanding balance, overdue status or ageing.

Why it matters:
It helps management focus collection efforts on the accounts that represent the greatest financial or recovery risk.
